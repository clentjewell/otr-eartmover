OTR tyre basics and codes

What is an off-the-road (OTR) tyre?

An OTR (off-the-road) tyre is a tyre built for earthmoving and mining machinery rather than road vehicles, covering rigid and articulated haul trucks, loaders, dozers, graders and excavators. OTR tyres are far larger and heavier than truck tyres, use different carcass construction (radial or bias) and are rated by load, speed and tread wear rather than a simple speed rating. The Australian OTR tyre market was worth about AUD 208 million in 2023, projected to around AUD 317 million by 2032 (source: Credence Research).

What does a TRA service code like E-4 or L-5 mean?

A TRA (Tire and Rim Association) service code tells you the machine type and tread depth a tyre is built for. E-4 is an earthmover code for rigid haul trucks, with extra-deep tread for wear life on abrasive haul roads. L-5 is a loader and dozer code with deep tread for cut and heat resistance in digging work. In our opinion as an independent trader, always confirm the code against OEM data and a competent person before ordering, since the wrong code can shorten tyre life or affect machine performance.

What do the letter and number in a TRA code mean?

In a TRA service code, the letter identifies the machine category: E for earthmover (rigid haul truck), L for loader and dozer, G for grader, among others. The number shows tread depth and pattern, with a higher number meaning a deeper, more aggressive tread for tougher wear or cutting conditions. In our opinion as an independent trader, matching the full code to the actual machine and application matters, so always confirm it against OEM data and a competent person before fitting.

What is a star rating on an OTR tyre?

A star rating (one to three stars) on an OTR tyre indicates its rated carrying capacity and speed capability for its size. More stars means a stronger casing, built to carry higher loads or run faster within that size and construction. It is useful alongside TKPH data, but in our opinion as an independent trader it should never be read alone. Always confirm the star rating, and load and speed limits, against OEM data and AS4457 before fitting to a specific machine and duty cycle.

What is the difference between radial and bias OTR tyres?

Radial OTR tyres run cooler, wear more evenly and give better TKPH and fuel performance, which is why most modern mining fleets run radial. Bias tyres have stiffer sidewalls, can be cheaper and can suit low-speed jobs or applications with heavy sidewall abuse. In our opinion as an independent trader, radial and bias construction must never be mixed on the same axle or dual assembly, as this causes uneven deflection and wear and is a safety issue under AS4457. Confirm the right construction against OEM data and a competent person.

TKPH and load

What does TKPH mean and why does it matter?

TKPH stands for Tonne-Kilometre-Per-Hour, the mean tyre load in tonnes multiplied by the average working speed in km/h. Heat is the main killer of OTR tyres, and every tyre carries a manufacturer TKPH rating. If a site's actual TKPH exceeds that rating, the tyre overheats, the casing weakens, and belt separation or heat related failure becomes far more likely. This is our opinion as an independent trader, so confirm your figures against current OEM data and AS4457.

How do I calculate TKPH?

TKPH equals mean tyre load in tonnes multiplied by average working speed in km/h. Use the tyre's average load across the full haul cycle, loaded and empty legs, not just the loaded figure, and the average speed over that cycle, not top speed. Compare the result against the tyre's manufacturer TKPH rating for its size and construction. This is our opinion as an independent trader, so confirm your working figures against current OEM data before relying on them.

How do I know if a tyre has enough TKPH for my site?

Compare your site's calculated TKPH against the tyre's manufacturer TKPH rating for that size and pattern, and build in a safety margin, commonly 10 to 20 percent, because real haul roads rarely run at a steady average. Hot climates, steep grades and long cycles all push required TKPH up. This is our opinion as an independent trader, not engineering advice, so confirm your figures against current OEM data, AS4457 and a competent person.

How does heat affect required TKPH?

Heat lowers a tyre's effective TKPH capacity, because rubber and casing strength drop as ambient and internal temperatures rise. A tyre rated for a given TKPH in moderate conditions may need a lower working load, or a higher rated tyre, on hot Pilbara style sites or through summer. Manufacturers publish heat de-rating adjustments for their TKPH tables. This is our opinion as an independent trader, not engineering advice, so confirm the adjusted figures against current OEM data and a competent person.

Fitment, mixing and safety

Can I mix radial and bias tyres on the same machine?

Not on the same axle or on a twin (dual) assembly. Radial and bias tyres deflect differently under load, which causes uneven contact pressure, uneven wear and unpredictable handling, and on a haul truck that is a safety issue. Different construction on different axles is possible on some machines but changes handling, so most operators standardise one construction per machine. This is our opinion as an independent trader; confirm against OEM guidance, AS4457 and a competent person before acting.

Can I mix brands or patterns on the same axle?

Within the same construction (all radial or all bias) and the same size, load rating and speed rating, mixing brands or patterns on an axle is common in trader-sourced fleets and generally acceptable. Keep to a compatible TRA family, for example all L-5. What you must not do is mix radial with bias on one axle. In our opinion as an independent trader, document any mixed setup and confirm it against OEM guidance, AS4457 and a competent person first.

What is AS4457 and why does it matter?

AS4457 is the Australian Standard for earth-moving machinery off-the-road wheels and tyres, covering maintenance and safety. It governs how OTR tyres and rims are inspected, mounted, inflated, demounted and handled. This work is genuinely hazardous and can cause serious injury or death, so it must be done by qualified people using current manufacturer instructions. We treat AS4457 as reference, not advice; confirm the current edition and your obligations with a competent person.

How long do giant mining tyres last?

On hard mining haul roads, giant OTR tyres commonly last in the range of 4,000 to 8,000 hours, though it varies widely with cycle length, load, heat, road condition and operator behaviour. Well managed, watered roads and disciplined operation push life toward the top of that band; rough roads and heat cut it short. These are indicative figures from trading experience, not a guarantee. Your own fleet data and OEM guidance are the better reference.

When should an OTR tyre be scrapped rather than repaired?

It depends on where the damage is and how deep. Tread and shoulder cuts that do not reach the belt or casing are often repairable; damage into the casing, belt separation, run-flat or heat damage, and bead or sidewall failures usually mean scrap. The casing’s retread eligibility also matters commercially. In our opinion as an independent trader this is a call for a competent tyre inspector against AS4457 and the manufacturer’s repair limits, not a decision to make from a photo.

What tyre size fits a CAT 992K?

The CAT 992K wheel loader typically runs 45/65R45 tyres, usually an L-5 tread for cut and heat resistance, the same size across all four positions. Heat-resistant compounds are common for Pilbara iron ore. This is general reference and our opinion as an independent trader, not engineering advice; confirm the size, load rating and fitment against the machine’s OEM specification sheet before ordering. Cost, market and buying

How much do mining tyres cost?

Mining dump truck tyres typically run from about AUD 8,000 to over AUD 30,000 each, with the largest ultra-class sizes reaching around AUD 69,000 (source: Credence Research, Australia Off-the-Road Tire Market). Price depends on rim diameter, star rating, tread pattern (TRA code) and whether it is radial or bias. A rigid haul truck on big rubber can carry more tyre cost than the truck's original tyres did new. This is our trading view, not a formal valuation, so get a quote for your exact size and duty rather than relying on a rule of thumb.

Why are OTR tyres so expensive?

OTR tyres cost what they do because of scale, engineering and low volume. A single earthmover tyre can weigh several tonnes, uses heavy duty steel belted radial or bias construction rated to carry hundreds of tonnes at TKPH loads, and is built to survive roughly 4,000 to 8,000 hours of heat, cutting and impact on a haul road. Production runs are small compared with car and truck tyres, so there is little economy of scale. This is our view as an independent trader, not a manufacturing cost breakdown, so confirm specifics with the manufacturer for your model.

Tier-one vs mid-tier OTR tyres: when is each the right call?

Tier one brands generally give the most consistent TKPH performance, casing durability and repairability, which suits high speed haul road duty where downtime and retreading matter most. Mid tier tyres can suit lower speed, shorter cycle or standby applications where budget matters more than the last percent of casing life. It is a duty cycle and budget decision, not brand loyalty. Confirm the tyre's TKPH and star rating against your site's actual load and speed before choosing; this is our trading opinion, not engineering advice.

What are typical lead times for OTR tyres in Australia?

Common sizes held in Australian stock can move in days. Less common sizes, large ultra-class tyres or specific tread patterns ordered direct from a manufacturer can take anywhere from several weeks to several months, longer again if shipped from overseas during high mining demand. Independent traders like Jewell Tyres often hold surplus and used stock that shortcuts factory lead times for urgent breakdowns. This reflects our trading experience only, so always confirm current availability for your exact size and pattern rather than assuming a standard timeframe.

Can I buy surplus or used OTR tyres safely?

Yes, provided the tyre has been properly inspected for casing damage, heat history and remaining tread, and it is matched correctly to your machine, rim and duty. Never mix radial and bias construction on the same axle or dual assembly. Mounting, inflating and handling any OTR tyre and rim is hazardous and must follow AS4457, done only by a qualified person. This is our trading opinion, not a safety certification, so always confirm the specific tyre's condition and history before fitting.

How big is the Australian OTR tyre market?

The Australian OTR tyre market was worth about AUD 208 million in 2023 and is projected to grow to around AUD 317 million by 2032, a compound annual growth rate of about 4.78 percent from 2025 to 2032 (source: Credence Research, Australia Off-the-Road Tire Market). Growth is driven mainly by mining and construction activity, particularly haul truck fleets in Western Australia's Pilbara region. These are indicative AUD conversions from the underlying USD figures, so treat them as a guide rather than an exact benchmark.
